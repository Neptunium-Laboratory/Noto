# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We are committed to making participation in the Noto community a respectful, welcoming, and constructive experience for everyone, regardless of background, identity, experience level, ability, or location.

We expect all contributors, maintainers, users, and community participants to help create a community where people can share ideas, ask questions, report problems, and collaborate safely.

## Expected Behavior

Participants are expected to:

- Communicate respectfully and professionally.

- Assume good intent while discussing disagreements.

- Focus feedback on ideas, code, documentation, and user experience rather than individuals.

- Welcome different viewpoints and experience levels.

- Give and receive constructive feedback.

- Respect project boundaries, privacy, and confidential information.

- Follow the contribution and security reporting processes documented in this repository.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, threats, or personal attacks.

- Sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention.

- Trolling, deliberate disruption, or repeated bad faith participation.

- Publishing private information without permission.

- Impersonating maintainers, contributors, or other community members.

- Misuse of project infrastructure or attempts to compromise accounts, systems, or user data.

- Sharing malicious files, unsafe code, credentials, or sensitive security details in public discussions.

- Retaliating against someone who reports a concern in good faith.

Disagreement about a technical decision is not, by itself, a code of conduct violation. The disagreement must remain focused on the project and be communicated respectfully.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to project repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, documentation, community channels, and other spaces where someone is representing Noto or participating in a Noto activity.

It also applies when behavior outside project spaces has a direct and serious impact on the safety or ability of community members to participate in Noto.

## Reporting a Concern

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, report it privately to the Noto maintainers through the security or private contact method documented in the repository. Do not include secrets, passwords, access tokens, or unnecessary personal information in a report.

A useful report includes:

| Information | Description |
| --- | --- |
| What happened | A factual description of the behavior or incident. |
| When it happened | Approximate date and time, including the time zone when relevant. |
| Where it happened | The issue, pull request, discussion, channel, or other location. |
| Who was involved | Usernames or other identifying information that is necessary. |
| Supporting material | Links, screenshots, or messages that help establish context. |
| Desired privacy | Any concerns about sharing the report with additional maintainers. |

Please do not use public issues to report harassment, threats, or security vulnerabilities.

## Response and Enforcement

Maintainers will review reports as promptly and confidentially as practical. They may ask for additional context, take steps to protect affected participants, or involve other trusted maintainers when necessary.

Depending on the severity and circumstances, maintainers may:

- Ask someone to stop a behavior.

- Remove or edit content.

- Close or limit participation in an issue, pull request, discussion, or channel.

- Temporarily or permanently restrict access to project spaces.

- Report serious threats or illegal activity to the appropriate authorities.

Enforcement decisions will be made with the safety and continuity of the community in mind. A person who reports a concern in good faith should not be punished for making the report.

## Unofficial Forks

This Code of Conduct applies to the official Noto project and its community spaces. Unofficial forks are independently maintained projects. Their maintainers are responsible for establishing, publishing, and enforcing their own community standards.

The Noto team does not provide support or moderation for communities, releases, issue trackers, or services operated by unofficial forks.

## Attribution and Changes

This document is inspired by established open source community conduct practices and has been adapted for Noto. The project may update this document as the community and its needs evolve.
